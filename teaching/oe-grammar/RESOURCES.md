# Historical Linguistics for Old English Study Resources

## Knowledge

- [Joseph Wright and Elizabeth Mary Wright, *Old English Grammar* (Internet Archive search/access hub)](https://archive.org/search?query=title%3A%28Old+English+Grammar%29+creator%3A%28Wright%29)
  Classic reference grammar with the older philological style that still shapes much Old English discussion. Use for: traditional accounts of Old English sound changes, inflection, and historical grammar terminology.

- [Bosworth-Toller Anglo-Saxon Dictionary (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/lex/master)
  Major dictionary for Old English. Use for: checking attested forms, semantic range, and older lexical evidence while following historical explanations.

- [Winfred P. Lehmann, *A Grammar of Proto-Germanic* (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/pgmc)
  Core reference for Proto-Germanic, including the comparative method and the major Germanic sound shifts. Use for: the big-picture map from PIE to Proto-Germanic.

- [Proto-Germanic Introduction: "The Linguistic Methods" (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/pgmc/1-introduction)
  Short, direct explanation of comparative method, internal reconstruction, and residues. Use for: how reconstruction works at a methodological level.

- [Proto-Germanic Phonology: relation of PGmc phonemes to PIE (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/books/pgmc/2-phonology)
  Useful compact statement of Grimm's Law and related consonant developments. Use for: consonant-shift orientation and examples.

- [Old English Online (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/eieol/engol)
  Practical Old English grammar and reading lessons. Use for: seeing how older sound changes show up in actual Old English forms.

- [Indo-European Lexicon (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/lex)
  Database of reconstructed PIE roots and daughter-language reflexes. Use for: comparing reflexes across languages and getting comfortable with reconstruction notation.

- [Indo-European Lexicon User Guide (University of Texas LRC)](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/guides/lex_user)
  Clear explanation of reconstructed roots, reflexes, and daughter languages. Use for: beginner-friendly reconstruction vocabulary.

- [MIT OpenCourseWare: 24.914 Language Variation and Change, Readings](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-914-language-variation-and-change-spring-2019/pages/readings/)
  High-quality course page that points to standard modern historical-linguistics reading, especially Campbell. Use for: expanding beyond the first lessons into mainstream method and theory.

- [Linguistic Society of America review of Lyle Campbell's *Historical Linguistics*](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/booknotices/?p=91)
  Useful confirmation that Campbell is a standard introductory textbook. Use for: choosing a deeper book when ready.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/linguistics](https://www.reddit.com/r/linguistics/)
  Broad community; useful for book recommendations and terminology questions, but quality varies. Use for: finding further reading, not for treating every answer as settled fact.

- [Linguist List](https://linguistlist.org/)
  Professional field hub with announcements and academic pointers. Use for: finding conferences, reading lists, and institutional resources.

## Gaps

- I do not yet have one especially good open-access source focused just on Old English i-mutation, a-mutation, and breaking as a single beginner sequence.
- I do not yet have a dedicated community source specifically for Germanic historical linguistics learners rather than linguistics in general.
- I should add more focused open-access resources for processes beyond the headline laws, especially syncope, assimilation, and analogy in Old English and Old Norse.
