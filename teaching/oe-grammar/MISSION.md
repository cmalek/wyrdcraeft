# Mission: Historical Linguistics for Old English Study

## Why
I am studying Old English and want a usable mental model of how Germanic languages change over time and how linguists reconstruct earlier stages like Proto-Germanic and Proto-Indo-European. I want this so that terms like Grimm's law, Verner's law, i-mutation, and reconstruction stop feeling like disconnected jargon and start helping me read Old English more intelligently.

## Success looks like
- I can explain the main Germanic sound changes in plain language and place them in a rough historical sequence.
- I can look at an Old English form and make a reasonable first guess about which earlier change may lie behind it.
- I can understand why reconstructed forms are written with an asterisk and what kinds of evidence support them.

## Constraints
- Keep lessons tightly scoped and quick to finish.
- Prefer examples from Old English and closely related Germanic languages.
- Build reusable reference sheets so the terminology stays stable across sessions.

## Out of scope
- Exhaustive Indo-European linguistics
- Full mastery of every Germanic sound law before building intuition
- Advanced disputes in reconstruction theory unless they directly help Old English study
